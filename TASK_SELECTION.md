# Task selection

**September 17 update:** the proposed HalfCheetah RMS-.72 independent-model check is now complete. Across eight new models, refinement gains +16.88 [6.06,28.29] over repaired BayesFP, below the fixed 26.19-point useful-effect threshold; conditioning versus projection remains inconclusive. Keep Pendulum as the primary motivating task. See [current results](RESULTS.md) and the [replication report](results/halfcheetah_replication/RESULTS.md). The original ranking and acquisition rationale below remain historical context.

Research and downloads were performed on 2026-09-14. **Current primary: Pendulum-v1 with the locally trained state diffusion policy. Secondary/fallback: HalfCheetah-v5 for finite-sampler comparisons; Push-T remains the contact-rich negative case.** The initial choice was Push-T; its bounded pilot did not show a useful gain. The completed follow-up confirmed useful Pendulum examples. [Round 3](round3/RESULTS.md) adds actual Walker2d/HalfCheetah diffusion experiments and BayesFP; its HalfCheetah sampler gain does not establish a conditioning-target advantage. Humanoid is not selected merely because it is high dimensional.

The modern v5 controller revisions, downloaded archive hashes, observation/action definitions, untouched smoke tests, episode-group splits and diffusion quality checks for the new locomotion tasks are in [round3/RESULTS.md](round3/RESULTS.md) and [round3/records/assets.json](round3/records/assets.json). Both tasks have 17 observations and 6 normalized controls; their control periods are .008 s (Walker) and .05 s (HalfCheetah). Their whole-chunk RMS limit is an actuator-command effort proxy, not mechanical energy. The older v3 asset research below is historical and was not silently substituted into these v5 runs.

The hypothesized mechanism is conditional, not a dominance theorem. Projection can also help: slowing an otherwise good approach can preserve progress, and returning a feasible output can outperform a finite-budget rejection refusal. If reward is smooth in action and the correction is small, the two distributions may have indistinguishable useful behavior. The paired pilot tests these possibilities.

## Push-T released asset and reproduction details

The authors provide [source and released experiment instructions](https://github.com/real-stanford/diffusion_policy), an [actual transformer checkpoint directory](https://diffusion-policy.cs.columbia.edu/data/experiments/low_dim/pusht/diffusion_policy_transformer/train_0/checkpoints/), and a [training dataset archive](https://diffusion-policy.cs.columbia.edu/data/training/pusht.zip). The selected checkpoint is [epoch 0850, reported score 0.967](https://diffusion-policy.cs.columbia.edu/data/experiments/low_dim/pusht/diffusion_policy_transformer/train_0/checkpoints/epoch%3D0850-test_mean_score%3D0.967.ckpt). It was actually downloaded: 143,687,633 bytes, SHA256 `fa302d345467ef5927d60f3fd7cdd222e1ee8fda52216ca64323a08e2ce700a8`. The dataset URL returned HTTP 200; it was not downloaded because no training is needed. No local dataset or checkpoint was reused.

Source revision: `5ba07ac6661db573af695b419a7947ecb704690f`. The unedited [environment implementation](https://github.com/real-stanford/diffusion_policy/blob/5ba07ac6661db573af695b419a7947ecb704690f/diffusion_policy/env/pusht/pusht_env.py) uses a 512×512 pixel workspace, 2D absolute pusher position targets in [0,512], 100 Hz Pymunk physics and 10 Hz control. The kinematic pusher drives a dynamic T object through contact. The [keypoint environment](https://github.com/real-stanford/diffusion_policy/blob/5ba07ac6661db573af695b419a7947ecb704690f/diffusion_policy/env/pusht/pusht_keypoints_env.py) supplies nine block keypoints and pusher xy; masks are removed as in the authors' runner. Evaluation uses legacy reset geometry, full keypoint visibility, two observations and eight executed actions. The loaded checkpoint, rather than the website's generic config, determines all policy settings.

This is an 8.96-million-parameter diffusion transformer, not an RL demonstrator. Frozen EMA weights, DDPM's original 100 inference steps, internal action/observation normalization and upstream action-time slicing are retained. The smoke test executes the original environment and `predict_action` source through a small local driver. Strict state-dict loading and action slicing assertions pass. This is a dependency port, not a claim of bitwise reproduction of the authors' Python 3.9 / PyTorch 1.12 stack. Pymunk 6.2.1 matches the upstream pin. Native physics, policy and reward source files were not edited; all 369 vendored source/assets hashes are recorded.

The original [dataset implementation](https://github.com/real-stanford/diffusion_policy/blob/5ba07ac6661db573af695b419a7947ecb704690f/diffusion_policy/dataset/pusht_dataset.py) splits sampling by episode, but fits normalization over its replay buffer. Thus this released artifact does not establish training-only normalization. We preserve its stored normalizer to define p and use new simulator seeds for the comparison. Any future imitation training must implement the stricter episode split and training-only normalization in the brief, rather than copy that normalization routine.

The measured eight-episode smoke test achieved mean maximum native reward 0.9724 and strict completion 2/8 within 300 controls (30 s). The author's checkpoint filename reports a maximum-coverage score, not a promise of a 96.7% completion rate. Our small sample demonstrates useful alignment behavior, not reliable completion or the full published benchmark. Detailed outcomes, including unsuccessful episodes, are in `results/smoke.json`.

Speed caps are physically viable for some states: holding the pusher stationary is feasible from reset, and development rollouts provide moving candidates below 300–400 pixels/s. Holding is not itself useful task performance. Safe policy support and subsequent quality are measured per snapshot in the paired experiment. No obstacle geometry or reward is constructed to penalize projection.

## Current primary: Pendulum with public TD3 demonstrations

The [official Pendulum definition](https://gymnasium.farama.org/environments/classic_control/pendulum/) has observation `(cos θ, sin θ, angular velocity)`, one torque in [−2,2], 0.05 s controls and 200-step episodes. Its continuous native cost penalizes wrapped angle, speed and effort. The selected chunk is **12 controls = 0.6 s**, followed by the same public expert for the remainder of a 10 s episode. Both one-sided initial torque envelopes admit useful swing-up behavior; this was measured rather than inferred from equilibrium feasibility.

The [released TD3 checkpoint](https://huggingface.co/sb3/td3-Pendulum-v1) was downloaded at revision `f200e871a887a68eec2265f1c6ff366cfbf40ea8`. Its 5,920,968-byte ZIP has locally verified SHA256 `9597e5a20703d74786afc8e5f48d98b378816ad8743ee62e2a278483bc565823`. Publisher-reported deterministic return is −195.99 ±119.03 over ten episodes. Our untouched Gym 0.26.2 environment/actor smoke gave -134.35 over seeds 0–9; these are different seeds, not a claim of reproducing the publisher's exact score. Source dynamics, reward and scaling were checked before collection.

The serialized metadata confirms 3 observations and 1 action. Saved configs have no observation-normalization setting or separate VecNormalize asset. The original stack is Gym 0.21.0, SB3 1.5.1a8, Python 3.7.10 and PyTorch 1.11.0; loading in modern SB3/Gymnasium requires a checked compatibility adapter or extracting the actor with its saved scaling. Do not silently substitute a modern environment and call it reproduction. The [original Pendulum source](https://github.com/openai/gym/blob/v0.21.0/gym/envs/classic_control/pendulum.py) was downloaded to verify timing and action semantics.

The follow-up compared π and reflected `π_ref(cosθ,sinθ,ω) = −π(cosθ,−sinθ,−ω)` from 40 identical near-downward snapshots, without action noise. The two controllers produced distinct coherent useful swings. The first 0.8 s one-sided torque constraint removed all expert support, so it was rejected in development; the shorter 0.6 s constraint retains support. The originally proposed speed-cap scan was not needed or claimed as executed.

We collected **320 paired initial-state groups / 640 complete episodes**, split groups 256/32/32 before making overlapping 12-action chunks, and fitted normalization only to training episodes. Both reflection partners stay in the same split. The 96,768 training chunks produced a modest state DDPM with 100-step sampling. Closed-loop imitation with 0.2 s execution intervals was useful (-331.58 mean return over 32 held-out group resets); a full 0.6 s execution interval was weaker (-555.75), which remains a stated limitation. The first-chunk/common-continuation comparison was validated separately.

The trained diffusion checkpoint and collected data were each reproduced byte-for-byte in an isolated training rerun. Confirmation found both base-policy swing modes in every state, 34% safe acceptance for either sign, no failures, and useful return improvements for conditioning and refinement. The constraint is a temporary externally supplied actuator envelope, not an inherent Pendulum hazard. [PENDULUM_CONFIRMATION.md](PENDULUM_CONFIRMATION.md) is the current protocol; [FIRST_EXPERIMENT.md](FIRST_EXPERIMENT.md) remains the unchanged original Push-T preregistration for auditability.

## Walker2d and Humanoid asset checks

[Walker2d-v5 documentation](https://gymnasium.farama.org/environments/mujoco/walker2d/) specifies 17 default observations (positions excluding root x, then clipped velocities), six torques in [−1,1] and dt=4×0.002=0.008 s. An approximately 0.8 s chunk requires 100 controls. The [released SAC-v3 actor](https://huggingface.co/sb3/sac-Walker2d-v3) has the same observation/action dimensions in its downloaded metadata but belongs to Gym-v3. Revision `82149cc39deff447604a2af02e735af3f0f9521a`, ZIP 3,236,549 bytes, publisher SHA256 `cb749324e176bd3aa3a18309bac05ca0e050f86aa461f4e4835c9be5c3ebc262`; checkpoint HEAD returned HTTP 200. Publisher return 3876.28 ±75.51 over ten deterministic episodes; not independently reproduced. No normalization artifact is listed, and inspected config does not request normalization. A stock SAC actor is not a learned mixture of slow and fast coherent gaits.

Current [Minari Walker2d expert data](https://minari.farama.org/main/datasets/mujoco/walker2d/expert-v0/) provides 1,000 episodes / 999,190 transitions collected using SAC after 25 million training steps, on v5. Its documented fast wide gait is one behavior. The data is a preferable modern training source to pretending that v3 weights are interchangeable with v5, but it does not by itself resolve the shared-state diversity requirement.

[Humanoid-v5 documentation](https://gymnasium.farama.org/environments/mujoco/humanoid/) specifies 348 default observations and 17 torques in [−0.4,0.4]. The older [SAC Humanoid-v3 release](https://huggingface.co/sb3/sac-Humanoid-v3) instead contains a 376-dimensional observation space. Revision `45673ecd6008435dbded233341b344057d1c3a61`, ZIP 7,459,026 bytes, publisher SHA256 `2207ceaadea95a63534d7ac283cbdbfb265b20a4f782c9efa1a9cbf34264ea09`; HTTP 200 verified. Publisher return 6251.93 ±35.95 over ten deterministic episodes; not independently reproduced. No normalization artifact/config setting was found. The original [Humanoid XML](https://github.com/openai/gym/blob/v0.21.0/gym/envs/mujoco/assets/humanoid.xml) fixes 0.003 s physics and the [environment source](https://github.com/openai/gym/blob/v0.21.0/gym/envs/mujoco/humanoid_v3.py) uses frame_skip=5, giving 0.015 s controls: 54 controls give 0.81 s. This resolves an inconsistent frametime number in the current prose documentation. [Modern Minari Humanoid expert data](https://minari.farama.org/main/datasets/mujoco/humanoid/expert-v0/) also exists; it is not evidence for crouching or running alternatives from the same state.

Both locomotion candidates would require either verified multi-behavior demonstrations or additional expert training. A bounded gate would compare two separately trained or speed-command-conditioned controllers on 24 shared recoverable simulator snapshots; measure speed, stride/contact patterns, survival and reward from each identical snapshot. Only after observing useful alternatives should one collect ~200 complete episodes per behavior, split by episode/initial-state group, and train a small diffusion imitation model. Gaussian torque noise around a deterministic expert does not satisfy that gate. Old Gym-v3 checkpoints additionally require mujoco-py/native-library compatibility; a checked v5 demonstrator is a cleaner alternative. Neither expensive path was started.

## Provenance and hardware

`records/candidate_sources.json` and `records/extra_sources.json` preserve primary URLs, revisions, metadata and hashes. Candidate RL checkpoint hashes are publisher LFS hashes after an HTTP availability check, not hashes of locally executed weights. `records/asset_manifest.json` records the downloaded selected checkpoint and source. Source code is MIT; the inspected external checkpoint/data listings do not state separate asset licenses, so no broader weights/data license is inferred. Dependency licenses are preserved from installed package metadata.

All executed work is on the requested workstation: Linux x86_64, Threadripper 9980X, 128 logical CPUs, about 377 GiB RAM, RTX 5070 Ti with 16,303 MiB VRAM, driver 595.84, compute capability 12.0. Initial sandbox-only NVIDIA and network checks failed because devices/network were hidden there; authorized host access worked. Existing GPU workloads were left running. A fresh local virtual environment uses Python 3.12.3 and PyTorch 2.7.1+cu128, consistent with [PyTorch Blackwell support](https://pytorch.org/blog/pytorch-2-7/) and [NVIDIA's compute-capability listing](https://developer.nvidia.com/cuda/gpus). GPU tensor inference actually ran. No driver changes, physical devices, outreach or publication were performed.
