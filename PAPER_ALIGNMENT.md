# Alignment with the supplied draft and code

The supplied repository was cloned separately and inspected at revision `e2b2546d948c04f63ae7232933c768956872a94f` from https://github.com/CadenzaCoda/safe-diffusion-mujoco. At that revision it contains an RL expert registry, downloading and evaluation scripts. It does not yet contain a trained diffusion policy or the proposed Langevin refinement implementation. Its modern Farama-Minari MuJoCo-v5 expert links improve the future locomotion data path; they do not demonstrate conditional gait multimodality. No files in that checkout were edited.

The ongoing Push-T experiment answers the motivating target question using the actual frozen policy output law and exact rejection. This remains a distinct empirical object from the draft's ideal demonstrator density p0 or the stationary distribution implied by a learned score. A finite trained sampler and its normalization/clipping need not reproduce either ideal exactly.

The new full-horizon workspace-curtain sets are convex, have nonempty interior when the initial-state/dynamics geometry permits it, and admit global Euclidean projection. They fit the draft's convex-oracle setting without a local convexification assumption. Actual membership and solver failures remain independently checked.

A positive rejection-versus-projection example would support the usefulness of truncation, not establish that a finite-budget Langevin implementation reaches it. Any later refinement test must retain rejection as the empirical reference and report approximation error, score evaluations, feasibility and task quality. For the Push-T transformer, a refinement adapter must preserve the joint 10-slot prediction and constrain the executable slots 1:9; directly treating the 8-slot executable marginal as though its score were available would be unjustified. The VP/DDPM-to-VE score conversion and normalization metric also need to be explicit.

The supplied LaTeX is a draft with proof TODOs. This experiment does not certify its convergence theorems, rates, nonconvex extension or universal statements about existing samplers. In particular, finite-budget mixing and finite-step feasibility need measurement, and empirical-measure convergence is different from convergence of a single output law in a multimodal setting. The experiment will report selected examples and negative cases without turning them into a universal reward guarantee.

The completed follow-up implements gamma=1/2 annealed projected Langevin on a newly trained state Pendulum DDPM. This avoids the Push-T executable-marginal score issue: all 12 predicted coordinates are the executable chunk. The score conversion and frozen level/step schedule are explicit in `src/pendulum_refine.py` and `PENDULUM_CONFIRMATION.md`. It gains 56.31 / 48.66 native return points over terminal projection in two mirrored held-out examples, with strict feasibility. Exact rejection gains 54.68 / 47.91 points.

The finite implementation remains measurably different from rejection: action-kernel MMD is 0.251 / 0.237, compared with rejection-versus-independent-rejection values 0.029 / 0.038. Therefore the measured behavioral win cannot be described as demonstrated convergence to the target distribution. The actual DDPM law also includes physical-boundary atoms from clipping, so the draft's density/no-boundary-mass assumption is not literally satisfied by that empirical base law. The added safety boundary at zero torque is separately measured; conditioning places no observed mass there while terminal projection places about 65% of chunks there.

The present example removes one major coherent swing direction and retains the other. It supports the boundary-concentration motivation, but not yet the claim that a finite sampler preserves the relative weights of several surviving safe modes. Report the examples as a controlled simulation mechanism study alongside the negative Push-T result, not as evidence for universal return optimality or a completed proof of the draft.
