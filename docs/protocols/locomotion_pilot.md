# Fresh MuJoCo comparison after the policy-quality gate

The first 0.4 s Walker2d model failed (32.8% survival, about 30% expert return); preserve it. One bounded refit uses 12 controls = **0.096 s**, yielding 98.5% expert first-chunk/common-continuation return and 100% development survival. HalfCheetah retains 8 controls = **0.4 s**; its refit achieves 85.6% expert return. Both pass the predeclared 80%-return/80%-survival gate. Data, original models and failed gates are retained.

Freeze the v2 DDPMs and 100-step samplers, with physical action clipping [-1,1]. There are 17 observations and 6 controls. Use fresh full MuJoCo `mjSTATE_INTEGRATION` snapshots: 32 seeds starting 103000, first 16 at reset and remaining 16 after exactly 1 s under the public expert. Four replicates/state; 128 independent proposals/replicate. Reject by the first feasible proposal, save every proposal, and record all refusals. The action chunks execute fully unless native unhealthy termination occurs. Continue with the same public expert through **4 s total** for every arm. Native rewards, failure conditions and physical timing are unchanged; terminated episodes contribute their accrued return, with zero remaining reward.

Constraints are externally supplied RMS **normalized actuator-command** limits over all H*6 coordinates, together with the physical action box. They are aggregate effort proxies, not a mechanical-energy estimate or a full-episode state-safety guarantee. The envelope is active for the initial chunk. Walker caps are .72 and .75; HalfCheetah caps are .70 and .72, selected for development acceptance rather than reward. From already bounded base samples the exact Euclidean projection is radial scaling. Refinement can leave the physical box internally, so use the exact intersection projection `clip(c*y,-1,1)` with c determined by the scalar KKT condition. This formula was checked independently against 36 conic programs; maximum RMS solution discrepancy 7.9e-6 and maximum float32 constraint excess 8.4e-8. Evaluation feasibility tolerance is 1e-6 RMS and per-coordinate.

Arms: first-feasible conditioning, full-horizon projection, the fixed 1024-step annealed projected-Langevin refinement, paper-derived BayesFP K=32, and BayesFP plus the same exact terminal projection. BayesFP's safety-only cost is squared excess RMS; it has no reward term. Strength is selected by minimum repaired MMD against development rejection, from [20,100,500,2000,10000]: 100 at each stricter cap and 20 at each milder cap. All strengths, state seeds and sampling seeds are in `configs/locomotion.json`. No native-return outcomes were used to choose these strengths.

Primary metric is native 4 s return. Primary new contrasts are conditioning-minus-projection and refinement-minus-BayesFP+projection at each task/severity: eight comparisons. Bootstrap 32 state clusters with 20,000 resamples and report individual 99.375% intervals, yielding a Bonferroni 95% family correction under the bootstrap approximation. Also provide descriptive 95% intervals. A useful advantage needs positive adjusted interval and at least 5% of the fixed development expert return: 168.94 points for Walker, 26.19 for HalfCheetah. Do not declare a win with rejection refusals or unsafe outputs. Return differences across tasks are not numerically comparable.

Also report native failure counts, progress, all raw unsafe outputs, final-repair effects, boundary concentration, acceptance by snapshot, budget cost, wall time, score evaluations, ESS/lineages and RBF action MMD (RMS distance, bandwidth .2). Label nearest-expert action-template occupancy as a family diagnostic, not proof of conditional multimodality. Both expert templates are generated from each exact snapshot. Actual unfiltered diffusion first-chunk behavior remains visible, including failures.
